# Legal checklist (read this before publishing anything)

I drafted the documents in `legal/` against what the app actually does. I am not a lawyer, and these are starting points,
not advice. Have a lawyer review them, especially the terms, the liability limit, the governing law and the copyright policy.

## Fill in every [bracketed item]
OPERATOR NAME, ADDRESS, CONTACT EMAIL, DATE, JURISDICTION and VENUE, HUB ADDRESS, LOBBY ADDRESS, RETENTION PERIOD,
COUNTRY/REGION of your servers, minimum age (13 or 16), liability cap amount, DMCA agent details.

## Things only you can do
1. **Decide who the legal operator is.** A person or a company (an LLC is common). The documents name "us".
2. **Register a DMCA designated agent** with the U.S. Copyright Office (small fee, renewed every 3 years). Without it you
   do not get the DMCA safe harbour for user uploads on the hub. The agent details must match COPYRIGHT.md.
3. **Publish the documents** on your website (the app opens its bundled copies, but people need a public link too) and link
   them from the hub and the installer.
4. **Keep the privacy policy true.** If you add telemetry, accounts, crash reports, or a new ad source, update PRIVACY.md first.
5. **Check trademark** for "Plunge" in the countries you care about before spending money on branding.
6. **Moderation.** Someone must actually read takedown emails and act on them.

## Known risks the documents cannot remove
- Mod loaders and DLLs that profiles bundle run on players' PCs. The app asks first, but one bad profile can still hurt someone.
- Game publishers can object to mods of their games, whatever these documents say.
- If anyone under 13 (or 16) uses the hub, children's privacy laws may apply.
